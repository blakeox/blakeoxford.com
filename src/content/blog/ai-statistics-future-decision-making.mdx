<ContentBand
  align="center"
  kicker="Research Findings"
  title="What Our Study Found"
  description="Confirming SPSS Results With GPT-4 and o3-mini Models"
  class="my-16"
/>

<p class="text-xl leading-relaxed">
  In our published study, we tested whether LLMs could replicate SPSS outputs across a range of
  statistical tests.
</p>

<div class="not-prose my-16 grid gap-6 md:grid-cols-3">
  <Callout variant="success" title="Strong on Fundamentals">
    <p class="leading-relaxed opacity-90">
      GPT-4 generally matched SPSS on correlations, t-tests, and regressions, with only minor
      rounding issues.
    </p>
  </Callout>
  <Callout variant="warning" title="Weakness in Complexity">
    <p class="leading-relaxed opacity-90">
      In MANOVA and factorial ANOVA, errors appeared: wrong degrees of freedom, inflated
      coefficients, and "significant" results where none existed.
    </p>
  </Callout>
  <Callout variant="error" title="Risk of Error">
    <p class="leading-relaxed opacity-90">
      These aren't trivial differences — in regulated domains, a single misreported p-value could
      have serious consequences.
    </p>
  </Callout>
</div>

<Callout variant="accent" class="my-12">
  <blockquote class="text-xl leading-relaxed font-medium italic md:text-2xl">
    Our conclusion then: LLMs could assist but not replace validated tools or expert oversight.
  </blockquote>
</Callout>
